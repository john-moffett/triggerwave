Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 10, 2026

Triggerwave ("we", "us", "our") operates the mobile game Quarterback Clash, also called QB Clash (the "Game"), on iPhone, iPad and Android. This page describes what data is collected when you use the Game, why, who it is shared with, and the choices you have.

1. Data We Collect

The Game never asks for your email address, phone number, contacts or location, and it has no ads and no tracking. It doesn't need a login: the first time you play, it creates an anonymous player ID for your installation (with Firebase Authentication's anonymous sign-in: no email, password or other account). If you choose, you can save your progress with an account you already have, as described below.

To run the Game, our server stores:

  • Your player ID, name and team: the anonymous player ID; the name you type, if you choose one (it's optional, and your team's name is shown if you leave it blank); and the team you put together: its city, nickname, colours and kit. Used to save your game and to show you to the players you duel.
  • Accounts you save your progress with, only if you use them: if your device is signed in to Game Center (iPhone and iPad) or Google Play Games (Android), the Game saves your progress with that player account, and you can also sign in with Google, Facebook or Apple in the Game's settings. Firebase Authentication links each one to your player ID and receives its ID with that service; from Game Center, Google Play Games, Google and Facebook, the name it has there; from Google and Facebook, the address of its profile picture; and from Google, its email address, which we don't use or show to anyone. From Apple, the Game asks for nothing but your Apple account's ID: no name or email address. Used only to save your progress and bring it back on a new device or after reinstalling, and for your photo if you choose one. You can unlink Google, Facebook or Apple in the Game's settings at any time; your progress stays.
  • Your photo, only if you choose one: the profile picture of a Google or Facebook account you've saved your progress with. Our server fetches it from Google or Facebook, has it checked (section 4) and keeps a copy, which other players see beside your name until you take it off in the Game's settings, unlink that account or delete your account.
  • Your game: your coins, gems, cards and their levels, trophies, league and stadiums, the players you've blocked, and when you agreed to the community rules. Used to save and load your game and to find you rivals.
  • Duel recordings: when you play a duel, a recording of your plays is uploaded so that other players can play against it: each throw (where you aimed and when you let go), the plays and defenses, what happened on each play and the score, your cards' levels, your league, your team and name, the app version and the platform. Used to run duels and keep them fair.
  • Reports and feedback you send: when you report a play, a name or a photo, or send feedback, from the Game's Help screen: what you wrote; the recordings of your last few plays (for a play report) or the name and player ID of the rival you reported (for a name or photo report); and your player ID and name, league, number of duels played, the app version, your screen size and the time. Used to look into what you told us, and to fix and improve the Game.
  • Purchase history: which packs you bought, with the store's transaction IDs (the App Store's, or Google Play's purchase tokens) and dates. Used to deliver each purchase exactly once, and to add any that weren't delivered.

To see how the Game is played, so we can fix problems and improve it, the Game also sends the following to Google Analytics for Firebase and Firebase Crashlytics. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, and wherever the Game can't tell your country, it sends nothing until you say yes when it asks:

  • How you play: game events such as the tutorial, duels started and finished and their results, stadiums reached, Snap Drops opened, upgrades, visits to the shop and purchases, and when and how often you play. Never your name or your player ID.
  • Crash reports: what the Game was doing when it crashed or hit an error, with the device model, operating system and app version.
  • An app-installation identifier: an identifier Google's services give the Game on your device, to count players and sessions. It isn't your device's advertising identifier, which the Game doesn't use on any device.
  • Approximate location: your country or region, which Google infers from your IP address.

You can turn analytics off, or on again, at any time in the Game's settings. While it's off, the Game sends nothing to Google Analytics or Crashlytics, and turning it off resets the Game's analytics identifier.

2. How We Use It

To run the Game: to save your game and bring it back with the accounts you choose, find you rivals and run duels, deliver what you buy, check names and photos before other players see them (section 4), and look into the reports and feedback you send. And, through the analytics and crash reports in section 1, to understand how the Game is played so we can fix problems and improve it. We don't use any of it for advertising, we don't build advertising profiles, and we don't sell it.

3. What Other Players See

When you duel, your rival sees your name and your photo (if you chose them), your team with its colours and kit, your quarterback with your name on his shirt, your trophies and league, and your recorded plays as they play against them. A recording can be played against by many players, for as long as it's used for duels. You don't have to use your real name or a photo of yourself, and please don't put other personal details in either.

4. Checking Names and Photos

Names are checked before other players see them: by a word filter in the Game and on our server, and then by OpenAI, a service provider, which reads the name and tells us whether it breaks our community rules. We send OpenAI only the name and a scrambled (hashed) form of your player ID, never anything else about you. OpenAI keeps what it is sent for up to 30 days to watch for abuse of its service, and under its terms for businesses doesn't use it to train its models. A name that doesn't pass is never shown, and the Game says it isn't available. If a name is reported, we may check it again, and a name that breaks the rules is taken away and your team's name shown instead.

Photos are checked the same way before other players see them: OpenAI looks at the picture and tells us whether it breaks our community rules (for example nudity, violence, hate symbols, rude gestures, drugs, or words in the picture that break the rules). We send OpenAI only the picture and the hashed form of your player ID, and OpenAI keeps them as it does names. A photo that doesn't pass is never shown or kept. If a photo is reported, we may check it again, and a photo that breaks the rules is taken off and can't be used again.

5. Reports and Blocking

If you report a rival's name or photo, they aren't told who reported them. If you block a player, their player ID and name are kept with your game so that you're never matched with them again; they aren't told either.

6. Data Stored on Your Device

The Game keeps your settings, a copy of your game and your last few plays on your device, so that it can start quickly and a play report can include them. If you've saved your progress with an account, it also keeps which accounts those are and the addresses of their pictures, to show them in the Game's settings.

7. Third-Party Services

We share data only with these service providers, which process it to run the Game, each under its own privacy policy:

  • Google: Firebase Authentication (your anonymous player ID, and the accounts you save your progress with, above), Google Sign-In and Google Play Games Services on Android, when you sign in with them, Cloud Firestore and Cloud Functions (our server, which holds the data in section 1); Firebase App Check, with Google Play Integrity on Android and Apple's App Attest on iPhone and iPad, which checks that requests to our server come from the genuine Game, to keep duels fair, and isn't used for anything else; Google Analytics for Firebase and Firebase Crashlytics (the analytics and crash reports in section 1, with their advertising features switched off); and Google Play, which processes in-app purchases on Android. Privacy and Security in Firebase, Google Privacy Policy
  • OpenAI: checks names and photos, as described in section 4. OpenAI Privacy Policy
  • Apple: processes in-app purchases on iPhone and iPad; App Attest (above); and Game Center and Sign in with Apple, when you use them to save your progress. Apple Privacy Policy
  • Meta: Facebook Login, when you choose to sign in with Facebook. The Game asks only for your public profile (your name and profile picture), on iPhone and iPad through Facebook's Limited Login, and the Facebook SDK in the Game has its automatic event logging and advertising identifier collection switched off. Meta Privacy Policy
  • Unity Technologies: the Game is built with Unity and handles purchases with Unity In-App Purchasing, which sends Unity technical diagnostics about the purchasing library itself, such as errors and how long store requests take. It does not send your player ID, name or payment details. Unity's analytics, crash reporting and performance reporting are switched off. Unity Game Player and App User Privacy Policy

We may also disclose information if the law requires it, or to protect the rights and safety of our players and ourselves.

8. In-App Purchases

In-app purchases are processed entirely by the App Store on iPhone and iPad, and by Google Play on Android, under their own terms and privacy policies. We receive confirmation of what was bought, with its transaction ID and date, never your payment information.

9. Apple and Google

Apple may share anonymous crash reports and app analytics with us if you have chosen to share them with app developers in your device settings. If you test the Game through TestFlight, Apple may also share crash reports, usage information and any feedback you choose to send with us, as described in Apple's TestFlight terms. On Android, Google Play may share crash reports and performance statistics with us from devices whose owners have chosen to share usage and diagnostics with Google. We use this information only to fix problems and improve the Game.

10. Children's Privacy

The Game is intended for players aged 13 and over and is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child under 13 has used the Game and you would like their information deleted, please contact us.

11. Keeping and Deleting Your Data

  • We keep your game while you play.
  • Duel recordings are kept while they're used to find players rivals, and deleted when they're no longer used, or with the rest of your data.
  • Reports and feedback are kept while we look into them and use them to improve the Game.
  • Our server's logs, which we use to find and fix problems, are deleted after 30 days. The record of game events our server keeps from them (duels and their results, what was earned, bought and spent, and when) is kept for 14 months under your player ID, to keep the Game fair and to see how it's played. It stays for those 14 months if your account is deleted, under a player ID that then belongs to no account.
  • Google Analytics keeps game events for up to 14 months, and Crashlytics keeps crash reports for 90 days. They aren't linked to your player ID; turning analytics off resets the identifier they're kept under.
  • Your photo is kept until you take it off, unlink the account it came from, or delete your account.
  • OpenAI keeps the names and photos it checks for up to 30 days (section 4).
  • Records of purchases are kept after your data is deleted, under an anonymous identifier, as financial records and so that nothing is delivered twice.

Deleting your account. In the Game, open Settings and choose Delete account, under Account. It deletes your game, your duel recordings, your photo and your reports from our server straight away, with the sign-in behind your player ID and the accounts you saved your progress with, and the Game's copy on your device. It can't be undone, and anything you bought goes with it.

If you no longer have the Game, email us at qbclashsupport@triggerwave.com with your player name and team so we can find it, and we'll delete the same. Deleting the app on its own doesn't delete them. What's kept after your account is deleted is in the list above: records of purchases, under an anonymous identifier, and the record of game events, for up to 14 months.

12. Your Rights

Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR in the EEA and UK, or California law), you may have the right to access, correct or delete your information, and to object to or restrict its use. You can delete your account in the Game (section 11), or contact us to make a request, with your player name and team so we can find your data.

Where the GDPR applies, we process your game's data to provide the Game you chose to play; check names, look into reports and keep logs in our legitimate interest in keeping the Game safe, fair and working; and send analytics and crash reports only with your consent.

13. Security and International Transfers

Data is sent over encrypted connections and stored with Google Cloud in the United States. Names and photos are checked by OpenAI, and analytics and crash reports are processed by Google, in the United States and other countries.
